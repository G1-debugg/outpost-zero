# Research sheet: Surviving the lunar night
Confidence: A = agency statement, B = reputable report, C = verify (sources disagree).

1. A lunar night lasts about 14 Earth days. [B, several reports on Chandrayaan-3]
2. Night temperatures fall to about -200 C. Reports vary from about -190 C to about -250 C near the poles. Use "about -200 C" and mark it approximate. [C]
3. Chandrayaan-3 (India) landed on Aug 23, 2023 near the lunar south pole (69.36 S, 32.34 E). [B, SpacePolicyOnline]
4. Its lander Vikram and rover Pragyan were put into sleep mode in early September 2023 with batteries fully charged and panels pointed to catch the dawn. [A/B, ISRO posts quoted in reports]
5. ISRO tried to wake them on Sept 21-22, 2023. No response came. ISRO said the mission had already met its main goals. [B]
6. Chandrayaan-3 did not carry radioisotope heater units (RHUs). A senior ISRO official said that with RHUs they "would be confident of a revival". [B, TBS News]
7. China's Chang'e-4, with radioisotope heating units, revived after about -190 C in 2019. [B, several reports]
8. Chandrayaan-3's lander measured the surface at about 50 C and a few millimetres below the surface at about -10 C. [B, ScienceAlert quoting ISRO]
9. RadLab (NASA OSDR) hosts real dose-rate data from the Chang'e-4 lunar lander's radiation instrument (LND). [A, RadLab release history]
10. RadLab dose rates are in microgray per hour (uGy/h). [A]

## Kid-ready lines
- "A night on the Moon lasts about 14 Earth days."
- "It gets colder than -200 degrees. Batteries and wires can freeze."
- "Some landers carry small heaters that need no sunlight."
- "India's lander slept through the night. It did not wake up."
- "Space missions can succeed even when a lander goes quiet."

## Game-mapping notes (not facts)
- Do not say Chandrayaan-3 "failed". ISRO said its main goals were achieved. Say "went quiet".
- Heater units are a design choice with a cost (mass, nuclear material). Keep the tone neutral.
