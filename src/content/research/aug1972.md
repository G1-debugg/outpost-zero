# Research sheet: The August 1972 solar storm
Confidence: A = NASA statement, B = reputable report quoting NASA, C = verify before use.

1. A huge sunspot region appeared on Aug 2, 1972 and erupted again and again for about 10 days. [A, Science@NASA "Sickening Solar Flares"]
2. The storm came between Apollo 16 (crew home in April 1972) and Apollo 17 (December 1972). No one was on the Moon. [A]
3. One coronal mass ejection (CME) travelled from the Sun to Earth in under 15 hours, a record that still stands. [A, NASA Science "Helio and You", 2025]
4. NASA's radiation health officer (Francis Cucinotta) estimated an astronaut on the Moon wearing only a spacesuit could have absorbed about 400 rem in the August 1972 storm. That is about 4 sieverts (1 sievert = 100 rem). [A, Science@NASA, 2005]
5. That is "not necessarily" deadly, but a moonwalker would need a quick trip home for medical care. [A, Science@NASA]
6. An Apollo command module's aluminium hull would have cut 400 rem to under 35 rem at the blood-forming organs. [B, Science@NASA via Universe Today]
7. Hull thickness (g/cm2): Apollo command module 7-8, Space Shuttle 10-11, ISS up to 15, future Moon storm shelters possibly more than 20 (polyethylene plus aluminium). [B, same article, C: confirm numbers in a current NASA source before showing them as exact]
8. The Moon has no atmosphere and no global magnetic field to deflect solar radiation. [A, NASA, David Hathaway quote in Science@NASA]
9. The 1972 storm also disrupted power grids and communications on Earth. [B, several reports]
10. NASA uses DONKI and its Moon to Mars Space Weather Analysis Office to watch the Sun for exploration missions. [A, NASA Science 2025]

## Kid-ready lines
- "In August 1972 the Sun had a giant storm."

- "Thick walls block more radiation. Regolith can make thick walls."
- "The storm reached Earth in under 15 hours. That is a record."
